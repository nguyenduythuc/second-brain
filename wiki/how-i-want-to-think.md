# How I Want to Think

A personal card — open it when I feel stuck or lost. Not theory to memorize, but
*one* move to repeat. Distilled from the 2026-06-17 discussion: from my wife's
skill, and from the time I caught a wrong conclusion myself. (Signature
self-talk phrases are kept in Vietnamese — those are the actual words that
trigger me.)

## My real problem

I conclude too fast, guess, and don't fully ground it in fact. The root: I
**collapse** *what I observed* and *what I inferred* into one, in a single
instant.

But I'm **not short on ability** — the time I rebutted a conclusion built on a
hidden wrong assumption *was* critical thinking. I *have* it; it just **doesn't
fire evenly**. I critique other people's claims better than my own (everyone
does). That's the part to train.

## One single move

> **Trigger:** when I'm about to lock in a conclusion and feel a flicker of
> unease (*lăn tăn*).
> **Rep:** ask myself — **"Is this a fact or am I guessing?" (fact hay đoán?)**
> (or write two lines: *"My conclusion: ___. Because: ___."*)

This adds no knowledge to memorize — it **installs a pause**, the very pause my
wife hands me from outside. The goal: build it on the inside.

Two additions from the SpaceX incident (2026-07, see [[wiki/metacognition]]):

> **Trigger 2 — speed + relish = red flag.** When a conclusion arrives
> *instantly* and *feels good* (the "thốt lên" moment), that is exactly when
> guess-probability peaks — real facts usually take effort to recall. Same
> question: "fact hay đoán?" *(the "feels good" part is the agent's inference,
> not yet confirmed by me)*
>
> **Move 2 — "nguồn đâu, search đã."** Before reasoning on top of a piece of
> news: where is the source? search first. Verify the input before building
> on it. (Learned verbatim from my wife: "thử search xem có thế thật không.")

## What my wife does (my highest-quality feedback source)

She asks "why" through several layers until one "why" makes me stall — that's
the layer where I *have no fact*. She is **separating observation from
inference**. I can run a small version of that on myself.

## Three things to remember when stuck

1. **I'm not short on understanding. I'm short on reps.** Reading more won't
   help; repeating will. See [[wiki/understanding-vs-doing]].
2. **Messiness is a beginner's sign, not proof I'm bad at this.** It's what an
   un-automated skill feels like from the inside.
3. **Don't fight it with willpower.** Not "try harder" but *trigger + small rep +
   feedback*. Willpower runs out and breeds shame; shame breeds avoidance.

## How to use the brain correctly (so it doesn't become a crutch)

Tell a crutch from a gym with one question: **after using it, am I *more* able
without it, or *less*?** The brain is a gym when: *I think, it pushes back* — not
the reverse. And because every discussion is recorded, the brain surfaces **my
recurring thinking errors over time**. The dependency signal to watch for: when I
ask it to think *for* me instead of *with* me.
