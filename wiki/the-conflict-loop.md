# The Conflict Loop

**2026-08-22, third disclosure of the day.** Thức gave the ten-year history
behind the daily arguments. This page is built around the **mechanism**, not
around who is at fault — deliberately. A page organised as a case against
either person would be less accurate and would give him a document to return
to for reinforcement. See the Boundary section of [[wiki/trust-repair]].

> ⚠️ **Single-source.** Everything here is Thức's account and cannot be
> corroborated. His wife would tell a different story, and in it many details
> would weigh more heavily against him. Every description of her carries this
> constraint; none of it is established fact.

## What outranks everything else on this page

**This is a relationship with violence in both directions, and a child is
watching.**

- **Wife → Thức:** *"đánh tôi rất mạnh và nhiều"* (hit me hard and often) over
  several years, with sustained verbal attacks. By his account this is
  **domestic abuse**. Notably he narrates it without ever once using that word
  for his own situation — a common pattern for men in this position, who are
  rarely believed and rarely name it.
- **Thức → wife:** *"vì cục tính đã đánh vợ"* (my temper — I hit my wife). Also
  abuse. Being hit first does not create a right to hit back; "hot-tempered" is
  a description, not a justification.
- **The child** witnesses the fighting and is **pulled into it** by his wife,
  who regrets it afterwards.

Both statements are true at once and neither cancels the other. The relative
severity is not something this brain can adjudicate from one side.

**The child is the most urgent fact in the entire story** — more urgent than
the trust question. That harm is accruing *now*, not at some future decision
point about the marriage. Vietnam's national child-protection hotline is
**111** {reported, from memory — verify}.

Ordering that follows: **safety floor → individual work → only then anything
about repairing the couple.**

## The mechanism — stated by Thức without noticing

In the fourth sentence of his own account:

> *"Dần dần tôi không muốn tranh cãi với vợ vì các lần conflict đó, **dẫn tới
> việc tôi hay giấu/nói dối vặt**."*
> (Gradually I didn't want to argue with her because of those conflicts, which
> **led to me hiding things and telling small lies**.)

```
conflict (money, high standards, his looseness)
  → he doesn't want to fight
     → he hides / tells small lies        ← conflict-avoidance strategy
        → she eventually discovers
           → conflict is worse than before
              → he hides harder, lies bigger
                 ↺
```

**Lying is not a moral defect that appeared from nowhere. It is a survival
strategy in an environment where telling the truth is punished severely.** And
it is self-defeating: each discovery makes her reaction harsher, which makes
concealment more attractive. A positive feedback loop, running ~9 years, whose
largest failure to date is *"no, there's nothing else."*

This does **not** dissolve that lie. It identifies what to actually repair: not
"be a better person" but **break the avoid-by-concealing reflex** — specific
and trainable.

## Why it locks

The loop has a symmetric second half:

```
she reacts severely to bad news
  → honesty becomes expensive
     → he conceals
        → discovery → "he cannot be trusted"
           → she becomes more vigilant and severe
              → honesty becomes more expensive
                 ↺
```

> **Neither can break this alone, because each one's fix requires the other to
> move first.** He can only risk honesty if the reaction softens. She can only
> soften if she believes he is honest. That is why ten years has not resolved
> it — not because two people are defective, but because the structure is
> **locked**. {inference}

This is the single most useful reframe available here: it moves the problem
from *character* (unfixable, unnegotiable) to *structure* (has known exits).

## What actually opens a locked loop

1. **A safety floor, as a rule rather than a feeling.** Both commit: any
   physical contact ends the conversation immediately, separate rooms, and
   **never in front of the child**. If either cannot hold it, they should not
   be in the same room during conflict. This is technique, not punishment, and
   it is the precondition for everything else.
2. **The one unilateral move he fully controls: stop concealing, regardless of
   her reaction.** The trap to name in advance — **the cost is paid up front
   and repeatedly before the loop turns.** He will tell the truth, get the harsh
   reaction, and conclude "honesty doesn't work." It does not work
   *immediately*; that is not the same thing. This is the rep, and it is
   expensive.
3. **Separate "I hear you" from "I agree."** Much of the escalation comes from
   acknowledgement feeling like concession, so neither side can afford to give
   it. {inference}
4. **An outside party.** Locked loops generally do not open from inside.

## Standard couples counselling is the wrong first step here

Correcting the advice given earlier the same day: **while physical violence is
ongoing, conventional couples counselling is typically contraindicated** — it
can escalate risk, and the person in the weaker position cannot speak freely
when they go home together afterwards. {reported, from memory — verify}

Individual work first, for both; if couples work happens later, it needs a
practitioner experienced with intimate partner violence, not general marriage
counselling.

## Rereading the Zalo episode

Corrected timeline (see the correcting source): the chatting happened **years
earlier**, lasted **days**, was deleted by him, and was motivated by
retaliation — *"nhiều khi tôi còn muốn cho vợ tôi nhìn thấy"* (sometimes I even
wanted her to see it). His wife **discovered** it early 2026.

{inference} Wanting to be seen is not the behaviour of someone seeking another
relationship. It reads as **a communication act by someone with no remaining
channel** — being hit, unable to leave, unable to be heard, doing something
visible enough to register. Still harmful, still his responsibility. But it
points at the actual deficit: **he had no way to say "you are hurting me" that
would be heard.** Repairing that is upstream of everything else.

## The thyroid hypothesis — concrete and testable

Thức had **thyroid cancer**: thyroidectomy plus radioiodine. He lives on
hormone replacement. Mis-dosed replacement — under or over — characteristically
produces **persistent fatigue, brain fog, poor concentration, forgetfulness,
loss of drive, low mood and irritability**.

Set that against his opening message: *frequent mistakes several times a day,
lazy thinking, no motivation, no aspiration.*

> That list matches poorly-controlled hypothyroidism almost item for item.
> {hypothesis — mechanism asserted from memory, NOT searched; an
> endocrinologist must confirm}

Action: **TSH and FT4**, and an endocrinology follow-up where he reports the
fatigue, concentration and mood symptoms — not only recurrence screening.

The distance from *"tôi trẻ con"* (I'm childish) to *"my hormone dose is off"*
is enormous, and the second has a blood test. This is the strongest single
argument in this brain for [[wiki/how-i-want-to-think]]'s Move 2 — *nguồn đâu,
search đã* — applied to one's own body.

## The blind spot

{inference} His mother came to help with the newborn and **left**. His parents
separately fought with his wife *"because they saw I was suffering."* Those are
**two independent outside observers who found the situation intolerable.**

Thức assigns fault to his mother — *"sao ko cố mà lại bỏ cháu"* (why didn't she
try harder instead of abandoning her grandchild) — rather than reading the
signal in her leaving.

Worth recording alongside it: he came out of cancer surgery and radioiodine,
*"nhiều khi ko tỉnh táo nổi"* (often unable to stay lucid), and cared for a
newborn alone. He narrates this as a minor detail. It is not one.

## Two verdicts facing each other

Good, and worth naming as the model: *"cô ấy nhịn nhiều hơn... tôi ko nhìn ra
đc sự cố gắng đó"* (she held back more; I couldn't see that effort). Specific,
accurate, repairable — the correct shape of self-assessment, and he produced it
himself.

Locked, and the thing keeping the loop alive — his closing line: *"tôi không
tính toán, không biết lo cho gia đình, hay nói dối; vợ thì quá khắt khe, quá
nóng tính, quá điên."*

> **Two verdicts about human nature, pointed at each other.** When each person
> believes the problem is *what kind of person the other is*, there is no
> specific behaviour left to negotiate, and nothing can change — because nature
> cannot be edited. Same identity-label error as [[wiki/five-guess-chain]],
> now aimed outward as well as inward.

## The option not yet named

Ten years, daily conflict, violence both ways, a child watching and being
pulled in. **Whether this relationship is repairable — and whether repair is
the lower-harm path for the child — is a live question, not a settled
premise.** This brain does not answer it and should not. But leaving it unnamed
while everything above is on the table would be dishonest. The variable that
must fall either way is **conflict exposure for the child**.
