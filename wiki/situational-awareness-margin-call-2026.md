# Situational Awareness LP Margin Call (Aschenbrenner / Citadel, 2026)

Institutional-scale case of [[wiki/margin-and-leverage-risk]], from the same
July 2026 AI/semiconductor rout that produced
[[wiki/korea-2026-margin-call-crisis]].

## What happened

`{fact ✓2026-08-02, web search — CNBC, FinanceFeeds, Tech Times, BeInCrypto,
Qz.com, method: cross-referenced search snippets across outlets, not
primary-source fetch (blocked 403 on all attempted direct fetches)}`

- **Leopold Aschenbrenner** (author of the "Situational Awareness" AI memo,
  ex-OpenAI) ran an AI-focused hedge fund, Situational Awareness LP, launched
  late 2024 with ~$225M.
- By mid-2026 the fund had grown to an estimated **$20–24B** in assets
  (one source's headline figure puts peak AUM at **$45B** — `{disputed:
  exact peak figure, $20-24B vs $45B — not reconciled, sources may be
  measuring different points/definitions}`), with a **+439% net return**
  through June 30, 2026.
- The fund ran **~4x gross leverage**.
- July 2026: the AI-infrastructure sell-off hit — Philadelphia
  Semiconductor Index (SOX) fell 28.6% from its June 22 peak.
- Three prime brokers (Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America)
  issued margin calls on the leveraged book.
- **July 30, 2026:** the fund sold its **entire public-markets book** (longs
  and shorts together) in a single block trade to Ken Griffin's **Citadel**,
  before market open — reportedly losing on the order of **$35B** in assets
  in the unwind.
- What remained: a **$5B stake in Anthropic** — a private holding, not part
  of the margined public book — and the fund continuing on, much smaller, as
  a private investment vehicle.

## Two questions the source article poses, answered by these facts

- **"Was Aschenbrenner actually wrong about AI?"** No — the surviving $5B
  Anthropic stake indicates the long-term thesis wasn't the failure point.
  The failure point was **~4x leverage meeting a short-term ~29% drawdown**;
  leverage forces liquidation on the lender's timeline regardless of whether
  the borrower's directional call turns out right. This is the "quyền được
  chờ" (right to wait) point from [[wiki/margin-and-leverage-risk]] made
  concrete at hedge-fund scale.
- **"Did Citadel rescue Aschenbrenner? Yes and no."** Yes, in the narrow
  sense that a single orderly block trade to one buyer avoided a chaotic
  multi-day fire sale into a falling market. No, in that it was still a
  forced, distressed sale at a steep loss — Citadel bought the portfolio,
  it didn't preserve the fund or the gains.
