# Reflection and Depth

Two sides of one process: **reflection (phản tư) is the *process*, depth (độ
sâu) is the *deposit*.** Reflect steadily over time and depth accrues on its own.

## Reflection (phản tư)

Not "thinking about everything," nor recalling what happened. It is the
deliberate act of **turning an experience back on itself to extract a
principle** — examining not just *what happened* but *why you reacted that way,
what you tacitly assumed was true, and what you'd do differently next time*.

The core distinction: **experience ≠ reflection.** Living through something 10
times is worth less than reflecting on it once. Many people don't have "20 years
of experience" — they have "1 year repeated 20 times," because they lived it
without distilling it.

**How to train it:**
1. **Predict → compare.** Write your prediction first, compare after. *Surprise
   is the signal that a model in your head is wrong* — hunt for the moments you
   were surprised.
2. **Ask "why" 3–5 layers down,** past the comfortable first answer.
3. **Write/say it to explain it** — externalizing forces structure.
4. **Actively look for where you're wrong** (steelman the opposite view).
5. **Premortem:** "Six months from now this failed — why?"

## Depth (độ sâu)

The difference between *knowing* and *understanding*. Surface = you can recite
it. Depth = you can derive it, connect it to other things, apply it to a new
situation, explain it to a child, and know *when it breaks*.

A test (the Feynman test): if you can't explain it in simple words, you don't
truly understand it. Depth is the product of **compression** — compressing many
examples into one principle, then decompressing that principle into new examples
you've never seen.

**How to train it:**
1. **Feynman test** — try to re-teach it in the simplest words; the stuck spots
   reveal the gaps.
2. **Choose few-but-deep** — depth is a deliberate trade against breadth.
   Re-reading beats skimming something new.
3. **Connect actively** — when learning X, ask what it resembles, contradicts,
   connects to. (This is exactly what the brain's `[[wiki/...]]` cross-linking
   does — linking is depth made physical.)
4. **Find the limits** — "when does this principle break?"
5. **Revisit over time** — depth accretes; it doesn't arrive in one pass.

## Important caveat

Understanding these two ideas (reading this page) does **not** make you better at
reflecting. That's declarative knowledge; the skill only comes from reps — see
[[wiki/understanding-vs-doing]]. The brain is a tool for both: the discussion
during ingest = reflection forced into words; wiki cross-linking = depth forced
into connections; `/query` + periodic lint = revisiting over time.
